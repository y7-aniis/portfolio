How might we help university students find events they enjoy and connect with people to attend them with?

02 / DEVELOPING THE CONCEPT

Finding an event was only part of the problem.

01

An easier way to find events

Event information was scattered across emails, social media and university websites. Our starting point was to bring discovery into one place.

02

Who would I go with?

We expanded the concept to address the social side of participation: finding a suitable companion and getting to know them before meeting.

03

Two ways to find a companion

Matching only within one event could leave students with too few choices. We added shared interests as another way to discover compatible people.

Start with an event you want to attend, then find someone going too.

01

Browse what’s happening

Event cards bring the activity, time, location and cost together, so students can compare options before opening a full description.

02

Know what to expect

The detail page keeps practical information close to the description. “Good to know” tags add context, such as whether newcomers are welcome and where to meet.

03

Find someone going to the same event

Students can join the event or look for a buddy from the same page. Finding a companion stays connected to the activity they are interested in.

From the event’s Find Buddy entry, students can explore a potential companion’s profile before connecting.

05 / USER TESTING

Testing & refinement

10 participants · 5 task scenarios · 2 revisions shown

01

A clearer profile entry

OBSERVED

The profile entry was overlooked; its arrow could be mistaken for an interest icon.

DESIGN REVISION

Named interest tags and a separate expand icon distinguish information from action.

02

Connect → Find Buddy

OBSERVED

The Connect label did not make its destination clear.

DESIGN REVISION

“Find Buddy” names the destination while retaining the icon and position.

06 / REFLECTION

What I’m taking forward

WHAT I LEARNED

Make the next action easy to recognise

The profile arrow and Connect label showed how small interface choices can interrupt a social flow. Clear entry points matter alongside the matching idea itself.

WHAT I WOULD TEST NEXT

Follow the connection beyond the screen

A prototype can reveal navigation issues, but it cannot show whether students will meet and attend an event together. Next, I would test a more functional flow with a broader range of students, tracking task completion and time alongside their feedback.
